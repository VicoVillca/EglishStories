The Blue Door 
Emma lived in a small, grey apartament in a big city. Every day was the same. 
She woke up, went to work at a quite library, came home, and watched TV.
She felt lonely sometimes, but she was used to it.

One Thursday, one her way home from work, she took a different street. She
wanted to see something new. The street was narrow and old. Suddenly, she 
stopped. There was a very small shop she had never seen before. In the
window, there was only one thing: a beatiful, old key. It was large and made
of brass. Next to it was a note: "Fits the blue door."

Emma was curious. She bought the key without thinking too much. It wasnt 
expensive. "Where is the blue door?" she asked the shopkeeper, and old man
with kind eyes. He just smiled and said, "You will find it when you need to."

The next day, Emma looked for blue doors everywhere. The door of her
neighbours home was green. The door of the cafe was red. She saw no blue 
doors.

A week later, Emma was in the park, reading a book. It started to rain heavili.
She ran to find shelter and saw a samall, hidden path behind some trees. At the
end of the path, there was an old garden wall. And in that wall, there was a 
door. It was painted a bright, cheerful blue.

Her heart beat fast. She took the from her bag. It felt warm in her hand.
She put it in the lock. It fit perfectly. She turned the key, pushed the door, and
walked in.

She didnt find a magical land. Instead, she found a wonderful, sunny
comunity garden. There were vegetables, flowers, and a few people
working. They looked up and smilled.

"Hello" said a women with a hat. "Weve been wainting for a new gardener.
That key means you want to join us."

Emma was surprised. She didnt know anything about gardening. But the 
people were friendly. They showed her a small empty patch of earth. "this
can be yours," they said.

That afternoon, Emma goes to the garden every Saturday. She has new friends. Her
tomatoes are growing tall. She still works at the library and lives in the grey
apartament, but her life is not boring or lonely anymore. Sometimes, the most
ordinal key can open the door to something special.
